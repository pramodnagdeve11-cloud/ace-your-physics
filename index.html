Mechanics

Projectile Maximum Range

R = (u² sin 2θ) / g

Horizontal displacement covered by an object launched with initial velocity \(u\) at angle \(\theta\) under uniform downward acceleration \(g\).

💡
Vikash Sir's Intuition: Maximum range occurs at exactly \(\theta = 45^\circ\) because \(\sin(2 \times 45^\circ) = \sin 90^\circ = 1\). Complementary angles (\(\theta\) and \(90^\circ - \theta\)) produce identical horizontal ranges!

Mechanics

Static & Kinetic Friction

f_max = μ_s · N | f_k = μ_k · N

Friction opposes relative impending or actual motion. Static friction adjusts dynamically from \(0\) up to its limiting maximum threshold.

💡
Common Pitfall: Static friction is self-adjusting! It only equals \(\mu_s N\) right at the point of slipping. Never plug in \(\mu_s N\) before verifying if the applied force exceeds it!

Energy

Work-Energy Theorem

W_net = ΔK = ½ m(v² - u²)

The total work done by all acting forces (conservative, non-conservative, and external) equals the exact change in kinetic energy of the body.

💡
Exam Strategy: When complex curved paths or variable forces make Newton's second law tedious, the Work-Energy Theorem solves problems in just two lines!

Electricity

Kirchhoff's Laws (KCL & KVL)

Σ I_in = Σ I_out | Σ ΔV = 0

KCL represents conservation of electric charge at any junction node. KVL represents conservation of electrostatic potential energy around any closed loop.

💡
Sign Convention Rule: Traversing in the assumed direction of current across a resistor gives a potential drop \(-IR\); travelling from negative to positive terminal inside a battery gives \(+\mathcal{E}\).

Optics

Lens Maker's Formula

1/f = (μ - 1) · (1/R₁ - 1/R₂)

Determines focal length \(f\) based on refractive index \(\mu\) relative to medium and radii of curvature \(R_1, R_2\) of optical surfaces with Cartesian signs.

💡
Submersion Trap: If a glass lens is submerged in water (\(\mu_{water} = 1.33\)), the relative refractive index drops, lengthening the focal length to approximately \(4 \times f_{air}\)!

Modern Physics

Einstein's Photoelectric Equation

K_max = hν - Φ = eV₀

Energy of incoming photon \(h\nu\) overcomes metal work function \(\Phi\), releasing photoelectron with maximum kinetic energy \(K_{max}\) stopped by potential \(V_0\).

💡
Fundamental Concept: Stopping potential depends strictly on light frequency \(\nu\), NOT intensity! Intensity only scales the rate of emitted electrons (photocurrent).
